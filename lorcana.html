How to play: beginner steps

Use a ready-to-play starter deck and learn one turn at a time. Follow the card text when a card gives a special instruction.

  1. 01

    Choose a starter deck

    Use a ready-to-play 60-card starter deck for your first game. Track lore and damage. Shuffle and draw 7 cards. You may set aside any number of opening cards, draw replacements, then shuffle the set-aside cards back into your deck.

  2. 02

    Ready, set, draw

    At the start of a turn, ready exerted cards, resolve start-of-turn effects, then draw. There can be a draw exception on the first player's first turn; follow the current Quick Start rules.

  3. 03

    Add a card to your Inkwell

    Once during your turn, you may put a card with an Inkwell symbol face down into your Inkwell. Ready ink is the resource you spend to pay card costs; exert ink by turning it sideways.

  4. 04

    Play cards

    Pay ink to play Characters, Items, Actions, and Locations. Characters normally cannot quest or challenge on the turn they enter play unless an ability such as Rush allows it.

  5. 05

    Quest or challenge

    Exert a ready character to quest and gain lore. Or exert it to challenge an opposing exerted character. Both characters deal damage based on Strength, and damage stays until an effect removes it.

  6. 06

    Race to 20 lore

    Track the lore you gain from quests and card effects. The first player to reach 20 lore wins. Read card abilities because they can change the normal rules.

Quick glossary

Know these basics

Ink

The resource you exert to pay for cards and abilities.

Inkwell

The area where eligible cards are placed face down as ink.

Quest

Exert a character to gain lore and move toward the goal.

Challenge

A character battle against an opposing exerted character. Compare Strength and mark damage.

First-game tip: Check the Inkwell symbol before placing a card face down as ink. Ravensburger's resources page has the newest Quick Start and comprehensive rules.

This is a beginner overview for a standard two-player game, not a complete tournament ruling. Card text, special formats, and current official rules can change a game’s details. Ask Feasted Gaming staff if you’d like a demo deck or help learning at the table.
