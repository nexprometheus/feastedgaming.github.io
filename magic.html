MAGIC: THE GATHERING

Magic: The Gathering

Magic is a strategy game where lands provide mana for spells and creatures battle for you. This guide covers a typical one-on-one game; Commander and other formats change some rules.

How to play: beginner steps

Use a ready-to-play starter deck and learn one turn at a time. Follow the card text when a card gives a special instruction.

  1. 01

    Set up

    Use two beginner 1-on-1 decks made for the same format. Shuffle and draw 7 cards. A typical 1-on-1 game starts at 20 life. Choose who goes first; the first player skips their first draw.

  2. 02

    Untap and draw

    At the beginning of your turn, untap your tapped cards, resolve beginning-of-turn effects, then draw a card (except on the first player's first turn).

  3. 03

    Play a land for mana

    You may play one land from your hand during your turn. Tap lands to make mana, the resource used to cast spells. Pay the colored and generic symbols shown in a spell's cost.

  4. 04

    Cast spells

    During a main phase, spend mana to cast creatures, artifacts, enchantments, sorceries, and other spells. Permanents stay on the battlefield; instants can often be played at other times.

  5. 05

    Attack and block

    Choose untapped creatures to attack and tap them. The defending player may block with untapped creatures. Unblocked attackers damage the player; blockers and attackers deal damage to each other.

  6. 06

    End the turn and win

    After combat, you get a second main phase, then an end step. Usually you win by reducing your opponent's life to 0; card effects can create other ways to win.

Quick glossary

Know these basics

Land

A card that produces mana. In most games, you can play one land per turn.

Mana

The resource made by lands and spent to cast spells.

Creature

A permanent that can usually attack and block. Creatures normally cannot attack on the turn they enter under your control.

Tap

Turn a card sideways to show it was used. A tapped creature normally cannot block.

First-game tip: Announce your actions as you go: “play a land,” “cast a creature,” or “move to combat.” Give your opponent a chance to respond before moving to the next step.

This is a beginner overview for a standard two-player game, not a complete tournament ruling. Card text, special formats, and current official rules can change a game’s details. Ask Feasted Gaming staff if you’d like a demo deck or help learning at the table.
